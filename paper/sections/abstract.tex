\begin{abstract}
For multi-user satellite free-space-optical quantum key distribution with
subcarrier-intensity-modulation BPSK and dual-threshold detection, we optimize
modulation depth, dual-threshold coefficients and the inter-user exclusion ratio under
unauthorized-receiver and beam-splitting security, a nonconvex problem coupling all $N$
pairs. A two-level decomposition with an exact outer/inner split is the oracle for a
learned controller, and we compare Kolmogorov--Arnold Networks (KAN), regularized
multi-layer perceptrons (MLP) and a linear map. Over an SGP4-propagated Walker shell at
Hanoi, adaptation yields $3.6\times$ the secure pair-time on the reported epoch,
$\rLongRatio\times$ pooled over $\rLongEpochs$; on a sparser shell it yields
$\rSparseRatio\times$ pooled, raising a static ceiling of $\rSparseStatHi$ secure
pair-step to a floor of $\rSparseAdaptLo$: survival rather than operation.
Three of four outputs are constant because the modulation depth saturates, so accuracy
is reported on the one with signal. KAN and the MLP are indistinguishable on $18$ of $20$
seeds; the MLP degenerates to the linear fit on $2$ and the KAN on none, which
this sample size does not establish ($p=0.29$). Retention saturates
for all three and is not ranked. What separates them is parameter count and the
closed-form rule extractable from the KAN, post-extraction $R^2 \approx 0.97$ in the
single-link setting, at a latency cost.
\end{abstract}
