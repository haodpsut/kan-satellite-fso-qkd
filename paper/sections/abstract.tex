We address adaptive parameter control for multi-user satellite free-space-optical
quantum key distribution with subcarrier-intensity-modulation BPSK and
dual-threshold detection. Extending the geostationary/low-Earth-orbit
architecture, we jointly optimize modulation depth, dual-threshold
coefficients and the inter-user exclusion ratio, a nonconvex problem coupling all $N$
Alice-Bob pairs under unauthorized-receiver and beam-splitting security. A
two-level decomposition with an exact outer/inner split is the oracle for the learned
controller. We compare Kolmogorov--Arnold Networks (KAN), regularized multi-layer
perceptrons (MLP) and a linear map. Over an SGP4-propagated Walker constellation at a
Hanoi station, adaptation yields $2.10\times$ the key and $3.6\times$ the secure
pair-time ($18$ vs $5$ pair-steps); on a sparser shell the best static
choice achieves no secure pair-step and adaptation only $2$ of $87$: survival, not
operation. Three of four outputs are constant because the modulation depth
saturates at its physical ceiling, so we report accuracy on the one carrying signal: in
the cluster setting KAN attains $0.010\pm0.001$ against $0.018\pm0.022$ for the MLP
at $43\%$ of its parameters. In the single-link setting, symbolic extraction gives a
closed-form threshold rule, post-extraction $R^2 \approx 0.97$ on one of five seeds. Closed-loop key retention saturates for every controller, including the
nine-parameter linear map, so we do not rank on it.
